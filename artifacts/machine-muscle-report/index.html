01 / USING A MULTI-USE STATION

Choose a movement,
not another machine.

1

Save the station once

Scan its QR code. For a new station, give it a name and add every supported exercise. Catalog matches or AI can suggest the list; you can correct it. Preview muscles before saving.

2

Compare the choices

Tap an exercise card. Its movement description and front/back muscle map update immediately. Lime means main muscles; blue means assisting muscles. Text labels carry the same information.

3

Open it for logging

Tap “Log [exercise] sets.” This remembers your choice and opens the set logger. Your active workout continues; a new one starts only when needed. Previewing alone does not log a set.

4

Train another movement

Return to the station, select its other exercise, and log those sets too. You keep one station record while the two exercises retain their own sets and AI programs.

Already saved it with only one exercise?

Open the station → Edit exercises → Add another exercise → Save exercises. Add only movements that your physical station supports. Removing one from this list does not erase its previous sets or saved program.

02 / DECIDING WHAT TO DO

Use your routine.
Compare the muscles.

If a current routine has an unfinished exercise on this station, the app marks it “Next here” and shows the number of completed sets. This is a routine-based cue, not an AI guess about what your body needs.

What opens when you return?

An exercise explicitly opened from the workout takes priority. Otherwise: the next unfinished routine movement on this station → last used exercise → the station’s default exercise. You can always choose another supported movement.

05 / IMPLEMENTATION & VERIFICATION

What changed behind it.

  • 24 offline exercise profiles. Movement descriptions and main/assisting muscle groups cover the built-in catalog. The original SVG model has front/back views and 18 muscle regions. Custom/unknown exercises use a general map from their saved groups without inventing a main/assisting ranking.
  • Hip Adductor added. Inner-thigh work can now be saved alongside Hip Abductor on a compatible station. Existing catalogs receive the missing exercise while existing edited records are preserved.
  • Maps throughout the flow. Preview while creating/editing a machine, choosing a saved station’s exercise, adding/editing routine exercises, and logging a workout.
  • Better choice context. Cards describe the motion, indicate selection accessibly, and show routine progress. The instruction video moves below exercise selection into an expandable section.
  • Remembering without accidental logging. Opening the logger persists the last exercise; preview taps stay read-only. Removing a movement clears an invalid remembered choice while keeping history and programs.
  • Safer station writes. Partial updates merge inside a transaction, so updating notes does not overwrite a separately saved choice or seat value.
  • AI follows the selected movement. A newly saved multi-use machine does not automatically generate a program for its first exercise. Program feedback is cleared when switching exercises so a correction for one movement does not leak into another.
  • Clear storage explanation. The station includes an expandable explanation of what is shared and what is separate. Previous sets are labeled by exercise rather than implying they came only from that station.

06 / Q&A

Practical details.

Should I do every exercise on a machine?

No. Follow the current routine’s marked movement or choose the target area you intend to train. Other supported movements are available; they are not an instruction to add extra work.

Why this matters: a list of capabilities should not look like a required checklist.

Is this a personalized muscle-activation simulation?

No. It is a schematic muscle-group guide for general exercise understanding. Color distinguishes main and assisting groups, not effort percentages, measured activation, or recovery. Setup and technique can change emphasis.

Why this matters: the diagram helps compare exercises without overstating what the app knows about your body.

Does it work when AI is unavailable?

Yes. The profiles and SVG diagram ship with the app. AI is optional for identifying an unknown station or generating/refining its starter program.

Why this matters: choosing an exercise should not depend on a network request.

Can the same movement have different notes on different stations?

Each station has its own notes. Within one station, those notes are still shared by its exercises. AI programs are separate for each machine/exercise pair.

Why this matters: this separates current station-level setup behavior from program storage.

Is the updated application live?

The implementation is in the local working tree and has passed the checks above. This public page is the explanation and screenshots; the application itself was not deployed.

Why this matters: publishing a walkthrough is separate from releasing the app.
