Screens

Tap a screen, read what it does

Home — today at a glance
  • One primary action. “Start Workout” is the biggest thing on screen — the plan's <2-taps-to-training goal starts here.
  • Calorie + protein rings mirror the food goals so you never open a second screen just to check macros.
  • Streak + last workout give the daily dopamine without charts overload.
Try it: tap Start Workout in the phone to walk the full gym flow.
Routines — two taps to training
  • Push / Pull / Legs cards with a “Up next” hint so the right one is always on top.
  • Each card shows exercise list, estimated time, and when you last did it.
  • Editing (drag to reorder, swap machines) lives behind the card — the main path is just “tap and go”.
Set Logger — < 10 sec per set
  • Previous performance is pre-filled (“prev 270×12” ghost text) — logging is usually just tapping ✓.
  • Tapping ✓ fires the rest timer toast automatically (try it on set 2 or 3).
  • Up next list keeps you oriented mid-workout; the blue QR button (bottom right) jumps to the scanner anytime.
Try it: tap a ✓ button to log a set and watch the rest timer appear.
QR Scan — the LA Fitness superpower
  • LA Fitness machines already carry QR stickers pointing at official videos (e.g. Life Fitness Training on YouTube).
  • Known URL → resolves instantly to the machine card (green toast at the bottom of the viewfinder).
  • Unknown URL → saved raw, you name it once (“Chest press by the windows”), and every future scan is instant.
Try it: tap the green “Life Fitness — Seated Leg Press” result.
Machine — scan → identify → learn → log
  • The official video embeds right on top — tap the thumbnail to open the real Life Fitness instruction video.
  • “My setup” is the personal layer: seat 4, your notes, your nickname for this exact machine at your club.
  • Last time shows previous sets, and one green button drops you into the logger.
  • The ★ favorites this machine so it surfaces first in routines.
Food — calories + protein first
  • Two bars on top: calories (green) and protein (blue). Carbs/fat are optional detail, not the headline.
  • Saved-meal chips make repeat meals one tap — “protein shake” shouldn't take 30 seconds twice.
  • Quick add is just name · kcal · protein. No barcode scanner or giant food DB in v1, per the plan.
History — progress you can feel
  • Week strip shows which days you trained — the streak view.
  • Weekly volume bars and PR callouts, not overwhelming analytics.
  • Body weight trend lives here too. All from local data — works fully offline.
Summary — the reward screen
  • Duration + total volume as the two big numbers.
  • PRs flagged in gold, plus what improved vs. last session.
  • Optional note (“try seat 5 next time”) is attached to the workout and to the machine.
  • Saved automatically, locally — no account, no network needed.
Design language

Dark, high-contrast, one-hand friendly

Dark theme by default (gyms are bright; phones between sets are used at arm's length). One green primary action per screen, blue for secondary/navigation, gold reserved for PRs. Big touch targets — everything on the main path is tappable with a sweaty thumb.

Q&A — decisions I need from you

Questions before building the real thing

1 · Should the rest timer auto-start after every logged set?

In the mock, tapping ✓ pops a 90-second rest countdown automatically. The alternative is a manual timer button.

Why it matters: auto-start is the fastest flow (zero extra taps), but some people find a ticking timer on every set annoying — especially on light warm-up sets. If you rest very differently between exercises (90s legs vs 60s arms), the timer should be a per-exercise setting rather than global.

2 · Lb only, or lb/kg toggle from day one?

The mock shows lb everywhere, matching LA Fitness plate-loaded and stack machines.

Why it matters: units touch every stored set, every "prev 270×12" hint, and every chart. Deciding now is cheap (store in lb, display per setting); retrofitting later means migrating history. If you'll only ever train in the US, lb-only keeps v1 simpler.

3 · When you scan a machine mid-workout, should it swap into the current routine slot or log as an extra exercise?

Example: your routine says "Leg Press" but the machine you scanned is the hack squat because leg press was taken.

Why it matters: this is the most common real-gym situation (machine occupied → improvise). "Swap for today" keeps history clean per exercise; "add as extra" is simpler to build but makes your leg-press history show gaps. The answer changes the workout screen's data model, so it's worth deciding before Phase 1.

4 · Should the instruction video open inside the app (embedded player) or jump out to YouTube?

The mock shows an embedded-looking thumbnail that currently opens YouTube in a new tab.

Why it matters: embedding keeps you in the flow (no app switching mid-workout) but YouTube embeds need network and occasionally break for some videos; jumping out is bulletproof but loses your place. A middle ground: embed, with "open in YouTube" as fallback. This affects whether the machine screen needs an iframe player component in v1.

5 · Protein target: is 180 g / 2,200 kcal roughly right for your real goals?

The mock uses placeholder targets. The home rings and food bars are built around whatever numbers you set.

Why it matters: if you're cutting vs. bulking, the home screen emphasis could differ (calories-remaining vs. protein-remaining first). Knowing your actual goal lets the default screen highlight the number you actually chase daily.
